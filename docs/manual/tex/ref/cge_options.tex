\cgeentry{cge_options}{Settings of the solver}

\cgesec{Description}

\cmd{cge_options} (from version 0.10.1) sets how the model is solved, as
GAMS's \cmd{option} statement chooses a solver; without options it displays
the settings. They are saved with the model and apply to every solve:
\manref{cge_solve}, \manref{cge_dynamic}, \manref{cge_sensitivity},
\manref{cge_optimize} and \manref{cge_loop}. \opt{lin}{solver()} chooses the
linear solver of Newton's steps: Mata's dense LU, the default, or a sparse LU,
whose memory grows with the number of entries of the Jacobian rather than with
the square of the number of equations. From version 0.14.0,
\opt{}{mcp()} chooses the method for constraints and inequalities: the
semismooth Newton method, the default, or the active-set method of earlier
versions. From version 0.16.0, \opt{tol}{erance()}, \opt{iter}{ate()} and
\opt{time}{limit()} set the defaults of \manref{cge_solve}, as GAMS's
\cmd{option iterlim} and \cmd{reslim} do, \opt{jac}{obian()} chooses the
Jacobian of Newton's method, and \opt{}{reset} restores every default. A
solve then reports GAMS's model and solver statuses
(\manref{cge_solve}).

\cgesec{Quick start}

The sparse LU for every later solve
\begin{stcode}
cge_options, linsolver(sparse)
\end{stcode}
The sparse LU for systems of 1,000 equations or more
\begin{stcode}
cge_options, linsolver(auto)
\end{stcode}
The active-set method for constraints
\begin{stcode}
cge_options, mcp(activeset)
\end{stcode}
At most 30 iterations and 60 seconds for each later solve, with a tolerance of
$10^{-12}$
\begin{stcode}
cge_options, iterate(30) timelimit(60) tolerance(1e-12)
\end{stcode}
Every default again
\begin{stcode}
cge_options, reset
\end{stcode}
Display the settings
\begin{stcode}
cge_options
\end{stcode}

\cgesec{Options}

\begin{optdesc}
\item[\optarg{lin}{solver}{dense|sparse|auto|python}] chooses the linear solver of
Newton's steps: \cmd{dense}, Mata's LU factorisation with partial pivoting
(the default); \cmd{sparse}, the sparse LU of section~\ref{sec:sparse};
\cmd{auto}, the sparse LU for systems of 1,000 equations or more and the
dense one below; or, from version 0.27.0, \cmd{python}, SciPy's SuperLU
through Stata's Python integration, an experimental prototype that works
only on Stata 16 and later with Python and SciPy (Stata 13 to 15 stop with
an error). SuperLU factorised the 40,190-equation Jacobian of the
250-activity benchmark model in 2.8 seconds against 16.4 for the sparse LU,
and whole solves of the largest benchmark models took 2 to 2.5 times less
time; below about 100 activities the calls to Python cost more than they
save. \manref{cge_solve} takes \opt{sp}{arse} or \opt{den}{se} for one
solve.

\item[\optarg{}{mcp}{semismooth|activeset}] (from version 0.14.0) chooses the
method for the constraints of \manref{cge_constraint} and the inequalities of
\manref{cge_equation}: \cmd{semismooth}, every constraint in
Fischer--Burmeister form, solved at once by the semismooth Newton method (the
default), with the active-set method if it fails; or \cmd{activeset}, the
smoothed conditions, then the model solved in the regimes they give, a
constraint whose other condition fails changing regime
(\secref{sec:cons-solution}).

\item[\optarg{jac}{obian}{exact|differences}] (from version 0.16.0) chooses
the Jacobian of Newton's method in \manref{cge_solve}: \cmd{exact}, the exact
derivatives with Broyden's updates (the default), or \cmd{differences},
central differences over groups of columns at each step, the fallback of
section~\ref{sec:newton}. Both give the same solution; differences are slower
and serve as a check.

\item[\optarg{hes}{sian}{exact|differences}] (from version 0.28.0) chooses
the Hessian of the Lagrangian of the nonlinear programmes of
\manref{cge_solve}: \cmd{exact}, from the second derivatives of the
objective and the equations, by forward differentiation of their compiled
code (the default), or \cmd{differences}, central differences of their exact
first derivatives, the method before. Both take the same iterations to the
same solution; on a cross-entropy problem of 400 variables, exact Hessians
took 4.1 seconds and differences 142.

\item[\optarg{sca}{ling}{none|ruiz}] (from version 0.33.0) chooses the
scaling of the linear systems of Newton's method and of the semismooth
method, beyond the scales of the variables and equations from the base:
\cmd{none}, the default, or \cmd{ruiz}, Ruiz's equilibration of each
Jacobian. The rows and columns are divided by the square roots of their
2-norms, ten times at most, until the norms are within 5 percent of 1
\citep{ruiz2001}; the scaled matrix is factorised and the scales are undone in
each solve. The Newton step is the same in exact arithmetic, the pivots and
rounding those of a better conditioned matrix: on a model whose variables
enter every equation with coefficients from $10^{-5}$ to $10^{5}$, the
condition number of the Jacobian fell from $5.4\times10^{8}$ to 3.9, and on
the SYNTHETIC SAM from 3,000 to 64. In the tests the solutions are the same
with and without it, and the semismooth method took one or two iterations
fewer with an import quota. It applies with the dense and the sparse LU, to
runs with foresight and to linked regions (the setting of the first region).

\item[\optarg{cond}{itions}{base|solve}] (from version 0.43.0) chooses when
the conditions (\cmd{$}) of user variables and equations
(\manref{cge_equation}) are evaluated: \cmd{base}, the default, once, at the
base, so that every simulation has the terms and elements of the base; or
\cmd{solve}, at the parameters of each solve, as GAMS evaluates them when a
\cmd{solve} statement generates the model. A shock to a parameter that a
condition uses then brings elements of user variables and equations into
their domains, or takes them out, and terms into sums and conditional
terms, at the next \manref{cge_solve} and in each scenario of
\manref{cge_loop}; the solves of \manref{cge_sensitivity} and
\manref{cge_nash} are those of \cmd{cge_solve}. An element that comes into
its domain starts from its definition, or its \opt{}{value()}, at the base;
one that leaves it is 0. Conditions on variables use their base levels.
Under \cmd{solve}, a variable or equation whose domain no element meets at
the base is allowed, if it is defined after the option is set.
\manref{cge_optimize} and \manref{cge_dynamic} evaluate the conditions at
the base. \manref{cge_results}, \manref{cge_listing} and \manref{cge_gams}
take each simulation with the elements it was solved with, and GAMS solves
the structure of the scenario; \manref{cge_report} shows the elements of
every simulation it reports. Back to \cmd{base}, the
model takes the structure of the base again. In a model from a SAM the
conditions are those of the user definitions: the built-in equations keep
the flows of the SAM, where a zero flow calibrates to a zero share that no
condition can switch on.

\item[\optarg{tol}{erance}{\#}], \optarg{iter}{ate}{\#} and
\optarg{time}{limit}{\#} (from version 0.16.0) set the tolerance, the
iteration limit and the time limit, in seconds, of \manref{cge_solve} when
it is not given them, as GAMS's \cmd{option iterlim} and \cmd{reslim} apply
to every later solve. The defaults are $10^{-10}$, 100 iterations and no time
limit. A solve that reaches its iteration limit or its time limit stops
there, without the fallbacks and continuation that follow other failures,
and reports solver status 2 or 3.

\item[\opt{}{reset}] (from version 0.16.0) restores every setting to its
default.
\end{optdesc}

\cgesec{Remarks and examples}

Both linear solvers give the same solution, to the solver's tolerance; they
differ in time and memory. The dense LU holds the Jacobian as a square matrix
and factorises it in time proportional to the cube of its size; the sparse LU
holds only its entries, chooses a pivot order once, which later solves of the
same model use again, and factorises each Jacobian in that order. Its first
solve of a model therefore costs more than its later ones. The full system of a
large model, with tens of thousands of equations, can only be solved with it.

\cgesubsec{Example 3: limits and statuses}

From version 0.16.0, an iteration limit set with \cmd{cge_options} applies to
every later solve. After two iterations the tariff removal has not converged;
a time limit of 0 seconds stops the solve at its first check. Each failure
reports its model status, 6 (intermediate infeasible), and its solver status,
2 (iteration interrupt) or 3 (resource interrupt), as a GAMS solver would;
after \opt{}{reset}, the solve converges with model status 16 (solved):

\cgesubsec{Example 4: conditions at each solve}

From version 0.43.0, \opt{cond}{itions(solve)} evaluates the conditions of
user definitions at the parameters of each solve. In a standalone model,
$X_i = w_i Y$ is defined where $on_i$ is 1, and $T$ sums it over those
elements; a shock switches element \cmd{c} on. Solved with the conditions of
the base, $X_c$ does not exist and $T$ stays 30; solved with those of the

\cgesec{Stored results}

\cmd{cge_options} stores the following in \cmd{r()}:
\begin{storedres}
\resgroup{Scalars}
\res{r(tolerance)} & the tolerance of \manref{cge_solve} (from version 0.16.0)\\
\res{r(iterate)} & its iteration limit\\
\res{r(timelimit)} & its time limit in seconds, missing for none\\
\resgroup{Macros}
\res{r(linsolver)} & the linear solver: \cmd{dense}, \cmd{sparse}, \cmd{auto} or \cmd{python}\\
\res{r(mcp)} & the method for constraints: \cmd{semismooth} or \cmd{activeset}\\
\res{r(jacobian)} & the Jacobian of Newton's method: \cmd{exact} or \cmd{differences}\\
\res{r(hessian)} & the Hessian of nonlinear programmes: \cmd{exact} or \cmd{differences}\\
\res{r(scaling)} & the scaling of Newton's linear systems: \cmd{none} or \cmd{ruiz}\\
\res{r(conditions)} & when conditions are evaluated: \cmd{base} or \cmd{solve} (from version 0.43.0)\\
\end{storedres}
