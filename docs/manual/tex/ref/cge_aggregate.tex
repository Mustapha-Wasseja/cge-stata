\cgeentry{cge_aggregate}{Merge SAM accounts into groups}

\cgesec{Description}

\cmd{cge_aggregate} merges accounts of the SAM of the current model into
groups and starts a new model from the result; any earlier mapping,
calibration and simulations are discarded. The groups are given in the
\cmd{group} column of a template written by \manref{cge_template}, or in the
option \opt{}{groups()}. Accounts that are not in a group keep their label.

Every cell of the new SAM is the sum of the cells it merges, so totals add up
and each group's balance is the sum of its members' balances. Flows among the
members of a group become a diagonal cell of the group; they are kept, unless
\opt{net}{diagonal} is given.

\cgesec{Quick start}

Merge the manufacturing and service activities and commodities
\begin{stcode}
cge_aggregate, groups(act_nag: act_man act_ser, com_nag: com_man com_ser)
\end{stcode}
Merge two households and remove every diagonal cell of the new SAM
\begin{stcode}
cge_aggregate, groups(hh: hhd npi) netdiagonal
\end{stcode}
Merge the accounts as the \cmd{group} column of a template says, and map the
result with the same template
\begin{stcode}
cge_aggregate using sam_roles.xlsx
cge_map using sam_roles.xlsx
\end{stcode}

\cgesec{Syntax}

Groups from a template
\begin{stsyntax}
\cmdb{cge_aggregate} \cmd{using} \argu{template} [\texttt{,}
\optarg{}{sheet}{string} \opt{net}{diagonal}]
\end{stsyntax}

Groups given as an option
\begin{stsyntax}
\cmdb{cge_aggregate}\texttt{,} \optarg{}{groups}{\argu{new}\texttt{:}
\argu{old} \argu{old} \dots\texttt{,} \argu{new}\texttt{:} \argu{old} \dots}
[\opt{net}{diagonal}]
\end{stsyntax}

\argu{template} ends in \cmd{.xlsx}, \cmd{.xls}, \cmd{.csv} or \cmd{.dta}.

\begin{opttable}
\optgroup{Main}
\optarg{}{groups}{spec} & the groups and the accounts they merge\\
\optarg{}{sheet}{string} & worksheet of an Excel template; default \cmd{accounts}, or else the first sheet\\
\opt{net}{diagonal} & remove the diagonal cells of the new SAM\\
\end{opttable}

\cgesec{Options}

\begin{optdesc}
\item[\optarg{}{groups}{spec}] lists the groups, separated by commas, each as
a new label, a colon and the accounts it merges, for example
{\frenchspacing\cmd{groups(hh: hhd npi)}}. An account may be in only one group. A group label
must be a valid Stata name; it may be the label of one of its members, but not
of another account. \opt{}{groups()} and a template may not be combined.

\item[\optarg{}{sheet}{string}] names the worksheet of an Excel template. The
default is the sheet \cmd{accounts}, which \manref{cge_template} writes, or
else the first sheet.

\item[\opt{net}{diagonal}] removes every diagonal cell of the new SAM,
including those of accounts that are not merged. Totals then fall by the
diagonal cells, and balances do not change.
\end{optdesc}

\cgesec{Remarks and examples}

Aggregation reduces the number of accounts of a SAM, for example to the
sectors of a policy question, or to a size at which every account has flows
large enough to calibrate the model. Each group takes the position of its
first member in the SAM. The number of accounts merged, the number of groups
and any diagonal cells are displayed, followed by the size, total and largest
imbalance of the new SAM, as \manref{cge_sam} displays them.

\cgesubsec{Example 1: merging accounts}

In the institutions layout of the synthetic SAM, the manufacturing and service
activities and commodities are merged, and so are the household and the
non-profit institution:
\stlog{agg_groups}
The new SAM has 14 accounts and the same total. Three of its diagonal cells are
non-zero:
\stlog{agg_show}
The diagonal cell of \cmd{hh}, 3, is the household's transfer to itself in the
original SAM, 1, and its transfer to the non-profit institution, 2. The other
two, the transfers of the enterprise and of the government to themselves, were
in the original SAM. The model accepts diagonal cells for households,
enterprises and the government, as transfers within the account, and the new
SAM maps:
\stlog{agg_map}
\manref{cge_map} reports a diagonal cell of any other account as a flow that
the model does not support.

\cgesubsec{Example 2: removing the flows within groups}

With \opt{net}{diagonal}, every diagonal cell of the new SAM is removed, and
the total falls by their sum:
\stlog{agg_net}
The SAM stays balanced: a diagonal cell is in both the row and the column of
its account.

\cgesubsec{Example 3: groups from a template}

Example~2 of \manref{cge_template} fills in the \cmd{group} column of a
template, aggregates the SAM with it and maps the result with the same
template. When the template has roles, the members of each group must have the
same role. A template written from a mapping that lists the non-profit
institution as a household and \cmd{ent} as an enterprise cannot merge them:
\stlog{agg_roles}
After aggregation, the template's accounts are no longer the accounts of the
SAM; \cmd{cge_aggregate using} the same template again is an error, while
\cmd{cge_map using} it maps each group with its members' role.

\cgesec{Stored results}

\cmd{cge_aggregate} stores the following in \cmd{r()}:
\begin{storedres}
\resgroup{Scalars}
\res{r(N_accounts)} & number of accounts after aggregation\\
\res{r(total)} & sum of all cells\\
\res{r(maxdiff)} & largest difference between an account's row and column totals\\
\res{r(N_diagonal)} & number of non-zero diagonal cells, before \opt{net}{diagonal}\\
\res{r(diagonal)} & their total\\
\res{r(N_blank)} & always 0\\
\end{storedres}

\cgesec{Methods and formulas}

Let $\bar X$ be the SAM of $n$ accounts before aggregation and $G$ the
indicator matrix of $m$ groups by $n$ accounts, with $G_{gk} = 1$ if account
$k$ is in group $g$ (an account in no group is a group of its own). The
aggregated SAM is
\begin{equation*}
X = G \bar X G', \qquad x_{gh} = \sum_{i \in g} \sum_{j \in h} \bar x_{ij},
\end{equation*}
so that each cell is the sum of the cells it merges. The row and column
totals of a group are the sums of its members' totals, and so is its balance:
\begin{equation*}
\sum_h x_{gh} - \sum_h x_{hg} = \sum_{k \in g} \Bigl( \sum_j \bar x_{kj} - \sum_i \bar x_{ik} \Bigr).
\end{equation*}
The diagonal cell $x_{gg}$ holds the flows among the members of $g$ and any
diagonal cells of its members. With \opt{net}{diagonal}, the SAM is
$X - \operatorname{diag}(x_{11}, \dots, x_{mm})$; each account's row and
column totals both fall by its diagonal cell, so balances do not change.
Aggregation is described in \secref{sec:aggregation}.
