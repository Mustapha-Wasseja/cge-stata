\cgeentry{cge_spa}{Structural path analysis of a SAM multiplier}

\cgesec{Description}

\cmd{cge_spa} decomposes the accounting multiplier of account \opt{}{to()}
with respect to an injection into account \opt{}{from()} into the paths along
which the injection travels through the SAM (Defourny and Thorbecke 1984). A
path is a chain of payments that passes through no account twice, for example
\mbox{\cmd{com_hlt > act_hlt > lab > hhd}}: spending on health services is received by
the health activity, which pays labour, which pays households. For each path
the command reports its direct influence, its path multiplier and its total
influence, and the share of the multiplier that it carries.

The SAM is the one mapped by \manref{cge_map}, and the endogenous accounts are
those of \manref{cge_multiplier}. The model in memory is not changed.

\cgesec{Quick start}

The paths from spending on health services to household income
\begin{stcode}
cge_spa, from(com_hlt) to(hhd)
\end{stcode}
As above, with paths of up to 8 arcs and a lower threshold, loading every path
found as data
\begin{stcode}
cge_spa, from(com_hlt) to(hhd) maxlength(8) threshold(0.0001) clear
\end{stcode}
The paths to labour income, with the government endogenous
\begin{stcode}
cge_spa, from(com_hlt) to(lab) endogenous(gov)
\end{stcode}

\cgesec{Syntax}

\begin{stsyntax}
\cmdb{cge_spa}\texttt{,} \optarg{}{from}{account} \optarg{}{to}{account}
[\argu{options}]
\end{stsyntax}

\begin{opttable}
\optgroup{Main}
\optarg{}{from}{account} & origin: the account into which the injection enters\\
\optarg{}{to}{account} & destination: the account whose income is decomposed\\
\optgroup{Search}
\optarg{max}{length}{\#} & largest number of arcs in a path; default 6\\
\optarg{thr}{eshold}{\#} & smallest direct influence kept, as a share of the global influence; default \texttt{0.001}\\
\optgroup{Accounts}
\optarg{endo}{genous}{accounts} & make exogenous accounts endogenous, as in \cmd{cge_multiplier}\\
\optarg{exo}{genous}{accounts} & make endogenous accounts exogenous, as in \cmd{cge_multiplier}\\
\optgroup{Reporting}
\optarg{}{top}{\#} & number of paths displayed; default 10\\
\opt{}{clear} & replace the data in memory with every path found\\
\end{opttable}

\opt{}{from()} and \opt{}{to()} are required.

\cgesec{Options}

\begin{optdesc}
\item[\optarg{}{from}{account} \textrm{and} \optarg{}{to}{account}] name the
origin, where the injection enters, and the destination. Both must be
endogenous accounts, and they must differ.

\item[\optarg{max}{length}{\#}] sets the largest number of arcs in a path; the
default is 6, and \argu{\#} must be at least 1.

\item[\optarg{thr}{eshold}{\#}] sets the smallest direct influence of a path,
as a share of the global influence in absolute value, below which the path and
its extensions are dropped. The default is \texttt{0.001}. \cmd{threshold(0)}
with a large \opt{max}{length()} finds every path, which can take long in a
large SAM; the search stops with an error beyond 100,000 paths.

\item[\optarg{endo}{genous}{accounts} \textrm{and}
\optarg{exo}{genous}{accounts}] choose the endogenous accounts, as in
\manref{cge_multiplier}.

\item[\optarg{}{top}{\#}] sets the number of paths displayed, largest total
influence (in absolute value) first; the default is 10.

\item[\opt{}{clear}] replaces the data in memory with every path found, in the
order of the display, with the variables \cmd{path} (the accounts of the path,
separated by \cmd{ > }), \cmd{length} (its number of arcs), \cmd{direct},
\cmd{multiplier}, \cmd{total} and \cmd{share} (the total influence as a
percentage of the global influence).
\end{optdesc}

\cgesec{Remarks and examples}

Structural path analysis treats the endogenous accounts of the SAM as the
poles of a graph. There is an arc from pole $i$ to pole $k$ when $k$ receives
part of $i$'s spending, with intensity $A_{ki}$, the share of $i$'s spending
that $k$ receives. For each path from the origin to the destination,
\cmd{cge_spa} reports
\begin{cgetable}{lp{0.70\linewidth}}
\toprule
Measure & Meaning\\
\midrule\endhead
\bottomrule\endlastfoot
direct influence & the product of the intensities of its arcs: the effect along the path with every other account held fixed\\
path multiplier & the feedback of the loops that start and end at the accounts of the path\\
total influence & the direct influence times the path multiplier\\
\% global & the total influence as a percentage of the global influence, the multiplier itself\\
\end{cgetable}
The total influences of all the paths from the origin to the destination add
up to the global influence. Like the multipliers, the paths assume fixed
prices and unused capacity.

\cgesubsec{Example 1: the paths from health spending to household income}

The health layout of the synthetic SAM is mapped as in
\manref{cge_multiplier}:

A unit injection into health services raises household income by 1.813, the
multiplier of \cmd{hhd} with respect to \cmd{com_hlt}:
\stlog{spa_paths}
The path through labour carries 64 per cent of the effect and the path through
capital 22 per cent; paths through the intermediate inputs of the health
activity carry most of the rest. The direct influence of the first path, 0.5733, is the
product of the shares along it: all of the spending on health services goes to
the health activity, which pays 18 of its 30 to labour, which pays 129 of its
135 to households. Its path multiplier, 2.0379, raises it to a total influence
of 1.168. The six paths found explain 97.66 per cent of the global influence;
the others have a direct influence below 0.001 times the global influence or
more than six arcs.

The results are stored in \cmd{r()}:

\res{r(N_paths)} & number of paths found\\

\end{storedres}

\cgesec{Methods and formulas}

Let $A$ be the matrix of average expenditure propensities among the endogenous
accounts and $M = (I - A)^{-1}$ the matrix of multipliers, as in
\manref{cge_multiplier}. An elementary path $p$ from origin $i$ to destination
$j$ is a sequence of poles $i = k_0, k_1, \dots, k_m = j$, none repeated, with
$A_{k_l k_{l-1}} \ne 0$ for each arc. With $P$ the set of its poles,
\begin{gather*}
I^D_p = \prod_{l=1}^m A_{k_l k_{l-1}}, \qquad
M_p = \frac{\det\bigl((I - A)_{-P,-P}\bigr)}{\det(I - A)} = \det\bigl(M_{P,P}\bigr), \qquad
I^T_p = I^D_p\, M_p,
\end{gather*}
the direct influence, the path multiplier and the total influence, where
$(I - A)_{-P,-P}$ leaves out the rows and columns of $P$ and $M_{P,P}$ is the
block of $M$ on $P$; the two expressions of $M_p$ are equal by Jacobi's
identity for complementary minors, and the command computes the second. The
global influence of $i$ on $j$ is
\begin{equation*}
I^G_{ji} = M_{ji} = \sum_p I^T_p ,
\end{equation*}
the sum over every elementary path from $i$ to $j$.

The paths are found by a depth-first search from the origin. A path is
extended along each arc to a pole not already in it; it is dropped, with every
extension of it, once $|I^D_p| < \text{\opt{thr}{eshold()}} \times
|M_{ji}|$, and it has at most \opt{max}{length()} arcs. When every intensity
is at most one in absolute value, as when the SAM has no negative cells, an
extension cannot raise the direct influence, so no path above the threshold
is missed. The share of the global influence explained is
$100 \sum_p I^T_p / M_{ji}$ over the paths found. Structural path analysis is
described in \secref{sec:spa}.
