\cgeentry{cge_synthetic}{Load the synthetic social accounting matrix}

\cgesec{Description}

\cmd{cge_synthetic} loads one of the synthetic social accounting matrices
(SAMs) bundled with \pkg as data in memory, in the wide form that
\manref{cge_sam} reads. It checks that the SAM is square and that its row and
column labels match, and reports its total and the largest difference between
an account's row and column totals.

The SAMs are SYNTHETIC: they were constructed for testing and teaching and
describe no country. The examples of this manual use them.

\cgesec{Quick start}

Load the standard synthetic SAM, replacing the data in memory, and read it
\begin{stcode}
cge_synthetic, clear
cge_sam, labels(account)
\end{stcode}
Load the SAM with a health sector and a social health insurance fund
\begin{stcode}
cge_synthetic, clear health
\end{stcode}
Load the unbalanced SAM used by the examples of \cmd{cge_balance}
\begin{stcode}
cge_synthetic, clear unbalanced
\end{stcode}

\cgesec{Syntax}

\begin{stsyntax}
\cmdb{cge_synthetic} [\texttt{,} \opt{}{clear} \argu{layout}]
\end{stsyntax}

\begin{opttable}[\argu{layout}]
\opt{nat}{ional} & national-accounts layout: exports sold by activities, taxes paid to the government (13 accounts)\\
\opt{ins}{titutions} & an enterprise, a non-profit institution and transfers among institutions (17)\\
\opt{mar}{gins} & margins netted out of the service activity (15)\\
\opt{ter}{ritorial} & a territorial correction of household consumption (17)\\
\opt{mult}{iproduct} & secondary production of a commodity (15)\\
\opt{non}{imported} & a commodity that is not imported (15)\\
\opt{}{sut} & supply--use layout: margin accounts, stock changes and re-exports (19)\\
\opt{}{health} & health services and a social health insurance fund (18)\\
\opt{unb}{alanced} & the institutions layout made unbalanced (17)\\
\opt{}{vat} & a value added tax account (16)\\
\opt{}{home} & home consumption (15)\\
\opt{}{trade} & an export-only cash crop and margins by market (19)\\
\opt{factort}{ax} & taxes on factor use and on factor income (17)\\
\opt{nest}{s} & energy and two types of labour, for nests of value added (19)\\
\end{opttable}

At most one \argu{layout} may be given; the default is the standard layout of
15 accounts.

\cgesec{Options}

\begin{optdesc}
\item[\opt{}{clear}] allows the data in memory to be replaced.

\item[\opt{nat}{ional}] loads the economy of the standard layout as many
national SAMs record it: exports are recorded as sales by the activities to the
rest of the world, and sales and direct taxes are paid directly to the
government account, so that the \cmd{tax_sal} and \cmd{tax_dir} accounts are
absent (13 accounts). Map it with \cmd{saltax(gov)} and \cmd{dirtax(gov)}; it
gives the same model as the standard layout.

\item[\opt{ins}{titutions}] loads a variant of 17 accounts with an enterprise
(\cmd{ent}) and a non-profit institution (\cmd{npi}). It includes transfers
among institutions, transfers to and from the rest of the world, factor income
from and to abroad, transfers within the government, and negative savings. Map
it with \cmd{households(hhd npi)} and \cmd{enterprises(ent)}.

\item[\opt{mar}{gins}] loads the standard SAM with margins of 30 netted out of
the service activity: its output is recorded as 120 and its use of its own
product as $-10$. Map it with \cmd{margins(act_ser)}.

\item[\opt{ter}{ritorial}] loads a variant of 17 accounts with a territorial
correction (\cmd{act_tc}, \cmd{com_tc}): residents' purchases abroad of 4,
non-residents' purchases of 9, and household consumption of the correction of
$-5$. It also has a fall of 2 in the inventories of \cmd{com_agr}. Map it with
\cmd{territorial(com_tc act_tc)}.

\item[\opt{mult}{iproduct}] loads the standard SAM with secondary production:
\cmd{act_agr} also produces 10 of \cmd{com_man}.

\item[\opt{non}{imported}] loads the standard SAM with \cmd{com_ser}, which
pays sales tax, not imported: its imports move to \cmd{com_man}.

\item[\opt{}{sut}] loads a supply--use layout of 19 accounts: margin accounts
\cmd{trd} and \cmd{trp} that collect margins from the commodities and buy
margin services, a commodity that is only imported (\cmd{com_fuel}), a
stock-change account (\cmd{dstk}), savings--investment labelled \cmd{s-i},
which \manref{cge_sam} renames \cmd{si}, and exports at least as large as
domestic output: \cmd{com_fuel} re-exports 2 of its imports, and \cmd{com_agr}
exports 105 against output of 100, with a fall in its stocks. Map it with
\cmd{commodities(com_agr com_man com_ser com_fuel)}, \cmd{savinv(si)},
\cmd{marginaccounts(trd trp)} and \cmd{stocks(dstk)}.

\item[\opt{}{health}] loads a layout of 18 accounts with health services
(\cmd{act_hlt}, \cmd{com_hlt}, neither imported, exported nor taxed) and a
social health insurance fund (\cmd{shi}) that receives contributions of 6 on
labour income, 2 from the government and 1 from abroad, buys 8 of health
services and saves 1. Map it with the four activities and commodities and
\cmd{insurance(shi)}.

\item[\opt{unb}{alanced}] loads the institutions layout made unbalanced.
Every non-zero cell is changed by up to 6 per cent, by a fixed rule, and
rounded to four decimals; the negative savings of \cmd{npi} and \cmd{gov} keep
their signs. Accounts are out of balance by up to 12. The examples and tests of
\manref{cge_balance} use it.

\item[\opt{}{vat}] loads the standard layout with a VAT account
(\cmd{tax_vat}) that collects 18 from the commodities and passes it to the
government, which returns it to households as transfers. Map it with
\cmd{vattax(tax_vat)}.

\item[\opt{}{home}] loads the standard layout with home consumption: the
household consumes 20 of \cmd{act_agr}'s output at home and buys 20 less of
\cmd{com_agr}.

\item[\opt{}{trade}] loads the standard layout with a cash crop,
\cmd{com_cash}, that \cmd{act_agr} produces and the economy exports entirely,
and margin accounts on \cmd{com_man}'s domestic sales (\cmd{mtrd}), imports
(\cmd{mtrm}) and exports (\cmd{mtre}). Map it with the four commodities and
\cmd{domesticmargins(mtrd)}, \cmd{importmargins(mtrm)} and
\cmd{exportmargins(mtre)}, and calibrate with \cmd{exportonly(com_cash)}.

\item[\opt{factort}{ax}] loads the standard layout with a payroll tax on labour
of 4, 3 and 6 paid by the three activities (\cmd{tax_pay}) and a tax of 7 on
capital income (\cmd{tax_fy}); direct taxes are 20 lower, so government
revenue is unchanged. Map it with \cmd{factortax(tax_pay:lab)} and
\cmd{factorincometax(tax_fy)}.

\item[\opt{nest}{s}] loads a layout with four activities and commodities,
including energy (\cmd{act_ene}, \cmd{com_ene}), two types of labour
(\cmd{lab_hi}, \cmd{lab_lo}), capital, and a payroll tax on skilled labour
(\cmd{tax_pay}). It is used to test nests of value added: map it with
\cmd{factortax(tax_pay:lab_hi)} and calibrate with, for example,
{\frenchspacing\cmd{nests(labour: lab_hi lab_lo, ke: cap com_ene)}} (see
\manref{cge_calibrate}).
\end{optdesc}

\cgesec{Remarks and examples}

The standard SAM has 15 accounts: three activities (\cmd{act_agr},
\cmd{act_man}, \cmd{act_ser}), three commodities (\cmd{com_agr},
\cmd{com_man}, \cmd{com_ser}), two factors (\cmd{lab}, \cmd{cap}), one
household (\cmd{hhd}), the government (\cmd{gov}), direct, sales and
import-tariff tax accounts (\cmd{tax_dir}, \cmd{tax_sal}, \cmd{tax_imp}),
savings--investment (\cmd{sav}) and the rest of the world (\cmd{row}). The
other layouts change it to exercise one feature of the model each: a layout
of national accounts that the mapping rewrites, an account role, or an
extension of the model.

Each SAM is stored as a CSV file and loaded as wide data: the string variable
\cmd{account} holds the row labels, and there is one numeric variable per
column account. Cell $(r, c)$ is a payment from the column account $c$ to the
row account $r$. The data are not yet a model: \manref{cge_sam} reads them.

\cgesec{Stored results}

\cmd{cge_synthetic} stores the following in \cmd{r()}:
\begin{storedres}
\resgroup{Scalars}
\res{r(N_accounts)} & number of accounts\\
\res{r(total)} & sum of all cells\\
\res{r(maxdiff)} & largest difference between an account's row and column totals\\
\resgroup{Macros}
\res{r(accounts)} & account labels, in the order of the SAM\\
\res{r(filename)} & path of the bundled SAM file\\
\end{storedres}

\cgesec{Methods and formulas}

The SAM file is found on the ado-path and read with \cmd{import delimited},
with the first column as strings and the others as doubles. The SAM must have
as many observations as account variables, and each row label must equal the
name of the variable in the same position, unless the label is not a valid
Stata name (as \cmd{s-i}, which Stata renames). With $x_{ij}$ the cell in row
$i$ and column $j$ of a SAM of $n$ accounts,
\begin{equation*}
\text{\res{r(total)}} = \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j=1}^n x_{ij}, \qquad
\text{\res{r(maxdiff)}} = \max_k \Bigl|\, \sum_{j=1}^n x_{kj} - \sum_{i=1}^n x_{ik} \Bigr| .
\end{equation*}
