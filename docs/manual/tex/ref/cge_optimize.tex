\cgeentry{cge_optimize}{Choose policy instruments to maximise an outcome or reach targets}

\cgesec{Description}

\cmd{cge_optimize} chooses the values of a few policy instruments to maximise
or minimise a report measure, or to reach targets for report measures,
solving the model at every trial value. From version 0.9.4, the objective and
the targets may also be expressions of the user language
(\manref{cge_equation}), and inequality constraints may be added, as in a
GAMS model solved by an NLP solver. An instrument is one element of a
parameter that \manref{cge_shock} changes, such as a tariff rate, or one
element of a variable that the closure fixes, such as a tax adjustment
factor. The optimisation starts from the instruments' current values, with
any shocks already applied, under the current closure. The optimum is stored
as a simulation, and the instruments are left at their optimal values as
current shocks, so that \mbox{\manref{cge_report}} and \mbox{\manref{cge_results}} show it.
From version 0.9.8, an optimisation with an objective reports marginals, as
the solution listing of GAMS's NLP models does: the shadow price of each
target and constraint, and the reduced cost of each instrument. From version
0.15.0, the derivatives of the objective, targets and constraints with
respect to the instruments are exact, as the derivatives an NLP solver such
as CONOPT or IPOPT takes from GAMS: they come from the model's exact Jacobian
at each solution, without solving the model again for each instrument.

\cgesec{Quick start}

The tariff on manufactures, between $-0.5$ and 1, that maximises the
equivalent variation of household \cmd{hhd}
\begin{stcode}
cge_optimize, instruments(tm(com_man)) maximize(welfare equivalent_variation hhd) ///
    lower(tm(com_man):-0.5) upper(tm(com_man):1)
\end{stcode}
Both tariffs that maximise it while tariff revenue stays at 8, storing the
optimum as simulation \cmd{two}
\begin{stcode}
cge_optimize, instruments(tm(com_agr) tm(com_man)) maximize(welfare equivalent_variation hhd) ///
    targets(government tax_import = 8) name(two)
\end{stcode}
The direct tax adjustment factor that keeps government savings at 3, with the
iterations displayed
\begin{stcode}
cge_optimize, instruments(TINSADJ) targets(government savings = 3) trace
\end{stcode}
Government purchases of health services and the direct tax factor that reach
targets for real GDP and government savings
\begin{stcode}
cge_optimize, instruments(qg(com_hlt) TINSADJ) targets(macro gdp_real = 310, government savings = 3)
\end{stcode}
The tariff that maximises government revenue while imports of manufactures
stay within 2 per cent of the base, written as expressions (from version 0.9.4)
\begin{stcode}
cge_optimize, instruments(tm(com_man)) maximize(YG) ///
    constraints(QM(com_man) >= 0.98 * base(QM(com_man))) lower(tm(com_man):0)
\end{stcode}

\cgesec{Syntax}

\begin{stsyntax}
\cmdb{cge_optimize}\texttt{,} \optarg{inst}{ruments}{list}
\{\optarg{max}{imize}{objective} $|$ \optarg{min}{imize}{objective} $|$
\optarg{targ}{ets}{spec}\} [\argu{options}]
\end{stsyntax}

\begin{opttable}
\optgroup{Main}
\optarg{inst}{ruments}{list} & the instruments; required\\
\optarg{max}{imize}{objective} & report measure or expression to maximise\\
\optarg{min}{imize}{objective} & report measure or expression to minimise\\
\optarg{targ}{ets}{spec} & targets for report measures or expressions\\
\optarg{cons}{traints}{spec} & inequality constraints\\
\optarg{low}{er}{spec} & lower bounds of instruments\\
\optarg{up}{per}{spec} & upper bounds of instruments\\
\optarg{}{name}{name} & name of the simulation that holds the optimum; default \cmd{optimum}\\
\optgroup{Solver}
\optarg{tol}{erance}{\#} & tolerance of each solve of the model; default \texttt{1e-12}\\
\optarg{iter}{ate}{\#} & maximum number of Newton iterations of each solve; default \texttt{100}\\
\optarg{grad}{ient}{method} & \cmd{exact}, the default, or \cmd{differences}\\
\opt{}{trace} & display the iterations of the optimiser\\
\end{opttable}

A \argu{measure} is a measure of \manref{cge_report}, written
\argu{table measure} [\argu{element}], for example
\cmd{welfare equivalent_variation hhd} or \cmd{macro gdp_real}. From version
0.9.4, an objective, a target or a constraint may instead be an expression of
the user language (\manref{cge_equation}), such as \cmd{YG - EG} or
\cmd{sum(c, PQ(c) * QG(c))}. An objective,
targets or both must be given; \cmd{maximize()} and \cmd{minimize()} may not
be combined.

\cgesec{Options}

\begin{optdesc}
\item[\optarg{inst}{ruments}{list}] lists the instruments, separated by
spaces. An instrument is
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\labelsep}{0.6em}
\item one element of a parameter that \manref{cge_shock} changes, written
\argu{parameter}\cmd{(}\argu{element}\cmd{)}, such as \cmd{tm(com_man)} or
\cmd{qg(com_hlt)}; the element may be left out when the parameter has only
one;
\item a scalar parameter, such as \cmd{trgr};
\item one element of a user parameter (\manref{cge_parameter}), such as
\cmd{w(com_agr)} or \cmd{sh(com_hlt,hhd)}, or a scalar one; or
\item one element of a variable that the closure fixes, such as \cmd{GADJ},
\cmd{TINSADJ} or \cmd{QFS(lab)}. A variable that the closure does not fix is
an error; it can be fixed by a swap (\manref{cge_closure}, \cmd{swap()}).
\end{itemize}
An instrument may be given only once.

\item[\optarg{max}{imize}{objective}] gives the measure or expression to
maximise.

\item[\optarg{min}{imize}{objective}] gives the measure or expression to
minimise.

\item[\optarg{targ}{ets}{spec}] gives targets, separated by commas, as
\argu{table measure} [\argu{element}] \cmd{=} \argu{value}, for example
\cmd{targets(government savings = 3, macro investment_real = 50)}. Without an
objective there must be one target for each instrument. With an objective,
the targets are equality constraints. From version 0.9.4, either side may be
an expression, as in \cmd{targets(sum(c, PQ(c) * QG(c)) = 50)}.

\item[\optarg{cons}{traints}{spec}] (from version 0.9.4) gives inequality
constraints, separated by commas, as a measure or an expression \cmd{<=} or
\cmd{>=} (or \cmd{=l=}, \cmd{=g=}) a value or an expression, for example
\cmd{constraints(QM(com_man) >= 0.98 * base(QM(com_man)), government tax_import <= 9)}.
Constraints need an objective. The display marks those that bind at the
optimum.

\item[\optarg{low}{er}{spec}] and \optarg{up}{per}{spec} give bounds, as
\argu{instrument}\cmd{:}\argu{value} pairs with the instrument written as in
\cmd{instruments()}, for example
\cmd{lower(tm(com_man):0) upper(tm(com_man):0.2)}. An instrument without a
bound is unbounded on that side. A lower bound must be below the upper
bound. From version 0.22.0, an instrument whose current value lies on or
beyond a bound starts inside it, by 5 per cent of a finite range or 1 per
cent of the bound's size, with a note; before, it stopped the optimisation.

\item[\optarg{}{name}{name}] names the simulation that holds the optimum;
the default is \cmd{optimum}. A simulation of that name is replaced.

\item[\optarg{tol}{erance}{\#}] sets the convergence tolerance of each solve
of the model; the default is \texttt{1e-12}.

\item[\optarg{iter}{ate}{\#}] sets the maximum number of Newton iterations of
each solve of the model; the default is 100.

\item[\optarg{grad}{ient}{method}] (from version 0.15.0) sets how the
derivatives with respect to the instruments are found. \cmd{exact}, the
default, takes them from the model's exact Jacobian at each solution, by the
implicit function theorem; \cmd{differences} takes them from central
differences of solves of the model, the only method before version 0.15.0,
kept for comparison and for objectives whose derivatives are not smooth.

\item[\opt{}{trace}] displays the objective and the largest gradient of the
free instruments at each iteration, or, with targets alone, the largest
scaled gap from the targets.
\end{optdesc}

\cgesec{Remarks and examples}

\cmd{cge_optimize} is meant for a few instruments: a tax rate, the volume of
a public service, a transfer. At each trial value of the instruments the
model is solved by Newton's method, from the solution of the trial before,
and the measures are computed at the solution. The objective and the targets
are values of the measures, such as amounts in the units of the SAM, not
percentage changes from the base; the equivalent and compensating variations
are amounts of base-price spending, not per cent of base consumption.
\Chapref{chap:optimize} states the problem and the algorithm.

\cgesubsec{Example 1: a welfare-maximising tariff}

The synthetic SAM is calibrated as in \manref{cge_solve}. The base values of
government revenue and its components, and a closure under which government
savings are fixed and the direct tax rates adjust, are
\stlog{opt_base}
The tariff on manufactures that maximises the equivalent variation of the
household, within $[-0.5, 1]$, with the iterations displayed:
\stlog{opt_max}
The objective is the equivalent variation in the units of the SAM; it is zero
at the start, the base. The gradient falls below $10^{-10}$ in six Newton
iterations. It is exact at each solution; the 29 solves of the model include
those of the differences of the gradient that give the Hessian. With
\cmd{gradient(differences)}, the method of earlier versions, the optimiser
needs 32 solves for a gradient of $7 \times 10^{-9}$, and the optimum
differs in the ninth digit. On the synthetic SAM the optimal tariff is about 30 per
cent, above the base rate of 10 per cent. The result depends on the SAM and
the elasticities, which here are synthetic and illustrative, and is not a
finding about any economy.

The optimum is stored as simulation \cmd{optimum} and left as the current
shock:
\stlog{opt_return}
The equivalent variation of 0.768 is 0.334 per cent of base consumption, as
\manref{cge_report} shows it. Government savings are fixed by the closure:
tariff revenue more than doubles and direct taxes fall by 38 per cent.

From version 0.9.4, the objective, targets and constraints may be written in

From version 0.9.8, the display gives the marginals. The constraint's is its

\cgesubsec{Bounds, failures and local optima}

An instrument at a bound whose gradient points outwards is held there, and
the display marks it \cmd{(at its bound)}. A trial value at which the model
does not solve counts in \res{r(N_failed)}; the next trial starts from the
last solution, and the optimiser steps back. The model must solve at the
starting values. With exact derivatives, the default from version 0.15.0,
the optimum is located to about $10^{-8}$ relative to the instruments' scale
or better; with \cmd{gradient(differences)}, derivatives come from central
differences of the solved model, and as a smooth objective is flat at its
optimum, it is located to about $10^{-6}$. An optimum may be local: other
starting values, set with \manref{cge_shock}, can be tried.

\cgesec{Methods and formulas}

With instruments $u$, the model's solution $x(u)$ under the current closure,
an objective measure $f$ and target measures $g_j$,
\begin{equation*}
\max_u\; f\bigl(x(u), u\bigr) \quad\text{subject to}\quad
g_j\bigl(x(u), u\bigr) = \bar g_j, \qquad \underline u \le u \le \overline u,
\end{equation*}
with $-f$ for \cmd{minimize()}. The instruments are scaled as $u = u^0 + s
\odot z$, with $u^0$ the starting values and $s_k = |u^0_k|$, or 1 when
$|u^0_k| \le 10^{-12}$. The target gaps are scaled as
$r_j = (g_j - \bar g_j)/\tau_j$, with $\tau_j = |\bar g_j|$, or 1 when
$|\bar g_j| \le 10^{-12}$.

\textit{Exact derivatives} (from version 0.15.0). At the solution $x$ of the
model at instruments $u$, with residuals $F(x, u) = 0$ and exact Jacobian
$J = \partial F/\partial x$ of the system solved, the solution moves with
instrument $k$ along
\begin{equation*}
\frac{dx}{du_k} = -J^{-1} \frac{\partial F}{\partial u_k},
\end{equation*}
by the implicit function theorem. $J$ is factorised once at each solution,
by the dense or the sparse LU of \manref{cge_options}; $\partial F/\partial
u_k$ comes from central differences of the residuals in $u_k$ with $x$ held,
with step $10^{-6}\max(1, |u_k|)$, which need no solve of the model. The
derivative of the objective, of each target and of each constraint along the
tangent $(dx/du_k, 1)$ comes from central differences of their values along
it, with step $10^{-5}\max(1, |u_k|)$, which need no solve either; the
explicit definitions that the reduced system of \manref{cge_solve} leaves out
are computed from the other variables at each point. With \cmd{gradient(differences)}, each derivative
comes instead from two solves of the model, as described below.

\textit{Targets alone.} Newton's method solves $r(z) = 0$. With exact
derivatives the Jacobian is that of the targets above; with differences it
comes from central differences with steps $h_k = 10^{-5}\max(1, |z_k|)$; the step
$\Delta z = -J^{-1} r$ is projected on the bounds and halved, up to 30 times,
until $\max_j |r_j|$ falls. The iterations stop, converged, when
$\max_j |r_j| < 10^{-11}$, and, with exact derivatives, when a step does not
reduce a gap already below $10^{-9}$, the precision of the solves; they stop
without converging when a trial value does not solve, when 30 halvings do
not reduce the gap, and after 50 iterations.

\textit{An objective.} A projected Newton method maximises
\begin{equation*}
F(z) = \frac{f}{\max(1, |f^0|)} - \lambda' r - \frac{\rho}{2}\, r' r,
\end{equation*}
with $f^0$ the objective at the start; without targets, $F$ is the scaled
objective alone. With exact derivatives the gradient is exact, and the
Hessian of the free instruments comes from central differences of the exact
gradient of the Lagrangian $L = F - \lambda' r + \mu' s$ below, with step
$10^{-4}$ and the multipliers held, less the penalty's part, $\rho\,
J_r' J_r$ over the targets and the constraints that bind, from the exact
Jacobian of their residuals; the iterations converge when the largest
gradient of the free instruments is below $10^{-10}$ without targets or
binding constraints and $10^{-8}$ with them, and near the optimum, where $F$ cannot
rise measurably, a Newton step whose predicted rise is below $10^{-12}
\max(1, |F|)$ is taken if it reduces the largest free gradient. With
\cmd{gradient(differences)}, the gradient comes from central differences
with step $10^{-5}$ (one-sided at a bound), the iterations converge when the
largest free gradient is below $10^{-8}$, and the Hessian comes from central
second differences with step $10^{-3}$. An instrument at a bound whose
gradient points outwards is held there. The
step is $-H^{-1}\nabla F$ when $H$ is negative definite, and the gradient,
divided by $\max(1, \max_k |\partial F/\partial z_k|)$, otherwise; it is
projected on the bounds and halved, up to 40 times, until $F$ rises. If no
halving raises $F$, the iterations stop, and they are counted as converged
if the largest free gradient is below $10^{-6}$; they also stop, converged,
when a step changes $z$ by less than $10^{-12}$, and after at most 100
iterations.

\textit{Targets with an objective.} By the augmented Lagrangian method, with
$\lambda = 0$ and $\rho = 100$ at the start, each round maximises $F$ and
then sets $\lambda \leftarrow \lambda + \rho\,r$; $\rho$ is multiplied by 10
when $\max_j |r_j|$ has fallen by less than three quarters since the round
before. The rounds stop, converged, when $\max_j |r_j| < 10^{-10}$, and after
at most 40 rounds.

\textit{Constraints} (from version 0.9.4). A constraint $h_k \le \bar h_k$ or
$h_k \ge \bar h_k$, with $\bar h_k$ a number or an expression, has the scaled
slack $s_k = (\bar h_k - h_k)/\sigma_k$ or $(h_k - \bar h_k)/\sigma_k$, which is
not negative where it holds, with $\sigma_k$ the scale of $\bar h_k$ at the base
(1 when it is near 0). By the method of Powell, Hestenes and Rockafellar the
maximiser takes
\begin{equation*}
F(z) - \frac{1}{2\rho} \sum_k \Bigl(\max(0, \mu_k - \rho\, s_k)^2 - \mu_k^2\Bigr),
\end{equation*}
and after each round $\mu_k \leftarrow \max(0, \mu_k - \rho\, s_k)$, starting
from $\mu = 0$, with $\rho$ raised as for the targets. The rounds stop,
converged, when the gaps in the targets and $\max_k |\min(s_k, \mu_k/\rho)|$,
which measures both the violation of the constraints and of complementarity,
are below $10^{-10}$. A constraint binds when $|h_k - \bar h_k| \le 10^{-6}
\sigma_k$. An expression, as an objective, a target or a constraint, is
compiled as a user equation is (chapter~\ref{chap:user}) and evaluated at the
solution of each trial.

\textit{Marginals} (from version 0.9.8). At the optimum, the Lagrangian of the
scaled problem is $L = F - \lambda' r + \mu' s$, with the multipliers the
updates give, $\lambda + \rho\, r$ and $\max(0, \mu - \rho\, s)$. The marginal of
a target or constraint is the change in the objective per unit increase in its
right side,
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\partial f^*}{\partial \bar g_j} = \pm \max(1, |f^0|)\, \frac{\lambda_j + \rho\, r_j}{\tau_j}, \qquad
\frac{\partial f^*}{\partial \bar h_k} = \pm \max(1, |f^0|)\, \frac{\pm_k \max(0, \mu_k - \rho\, s_k)}{\sigma_k},
\end{equation*}
with the sign of the objective ($-$ for \cmd{minimize()}) and $\pm_k$, $+$ for
$h_k \le \bar h_k$ and $-$ for $h_k \ge \bar h_k$, as the marginal of an
equation in GAMS's NLP models. The reduced cost of an instrument at a bound is
$\max(1, |f^0|)\, (\partial L / \partial z_k) / s_k$, from the exact gradient
of the augmented objective (central differences of it with
\cmd{gradient(differences)}), whose gradient at the optimum is that of $L$;
within its bounds it is zero. The test suite checks each against the change
in the optimum when the right side or the bound moves, and the exact
derivatives against differences and against problems whose optimum is known
in closed form (\texttt{tests/test\_optgrad.do}).

\Chapref{chap:optimize} gives the details.
