\section{What the package does}

\pkg builds, calibrates and solves computable general equilibrium (CGE)
models in Stata. The built-in model uses a social accounting matrix (SAM),
supports static and dynamic simulations, and can be linked to other regions
through bilateral trade. Users can also write standalone models with their
own sets, parameters, variables and equations. The built-in SAM-based model
follows the IFPRI and PEP traditions \citep{lofgren2002,decaluwe2013}; its
equations are stated in full in Part~\ref{part:model}:
\begin{itemize}
\item production with CES or Cobb--Douglas value added and Leontief
intermediate inputs, with an optional CES nest between them; activities may
produce several commodities;
\item trade through a CET function between exports and domestic sales and an
Armington CES function between imports and domestic goods;
\item households with Cobb--Douglas or linear expenditure system demand,
enterprises, the government, savings--investment and the rest of the world,
with transfers among them;
\item direct, sales, import, export and activity taxes, taxes on factors, and
VAT with input credits;
\item closures for the external account, the government, savings--investment,
the numeraire and each factor.
\end{itemize}
Extensions cover public debt, home consumption, finite export demand, margins
by market, labour-market regimes, a social health insurance fund, factor
efficiency linked to health services, health outcomes, nests of value added,
the aggregation of output from several activities, and limits that bind in
some scenarios and not in others. The model can be solved for one period,
as a recursive dynamic sequence, or with perfect foresight and user-specified
forward-looking equations (\manref{cge_dynamic}). Calibrated regional models
can be solved together with bilateral trade (\manref{cge_region}).
Results can be passed to household survey
data: poverty for several lines, inequality, incidence by decile and the
growth incidence curve, each household's welfare, the contribution of each
channel to every change, and fiscal incidence as the Commitment to Equity
method measures it, with survey weights that follow the model, standard
errors from the survey design and ranges across draws of the elasticities.
The sensitivity of results to the elasticities can be measured; policy
instruments can be chosen to maximise an outcome or reach targets; and users
can add their own variables, equations, sets and parameters, with conditions
and inequalities paired with variables as in GAMS. Tools read, aggregate, balance and
analyse the SAM, and a library gives published elasticities with their
sources. The model can also be written as a GAMS program, solved there and
brought back.

Standalone models support equilibrium and complementarity systems and
nonlinear optimisation, including integer or binary variables
(\manref{cge_model}, \manref{cge_solve}). \manref{cge_nash} adds Nash and
Stackelberg games, and \manref{cge_modlib} supplies ready-to-run examples,
including a Ramsey growth model. Perfect foresight solves periods together;
any forward-looking behaviour or intertemporal objective must be specified
in the user's equations or optimisation model. Elasticities remain inputs
supplied by the user or selected from the sourced library.

The built-in solvers run in Stata and Mata on Stata 13 or later. Optional,
experimental links to SciPy's SuperLU and HiGHS require Stata 16 or later
with Python and SciPy, and the links to IPOPT and SCIP the Python packages
cyipopt and PySCIPOpt as well (\manref{cge_options}, \manref{cge_solve}). Running an
exported model in GAMS requires GAMS and the selected solver.

\section{Installation}

The package needs Stata 13 or later and runs on Stata/IC. Install it from
GitHub (tested with StataNow 19.5):
\begin{stcode}
. net install cge, from("https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Mustapha-Wasseja/cge-stata/main/") replace
\end{stcode}
Stata 13 cannot connect to GitHub over HTTPS, because its TLS version is too
old. With Stata 13, download or clone the repository and install from the
folder:
\begin{stcode}
. net install cge, from("C:/path/to/cge-stata") replace
\end{stcode}
\cmd{help cge} lists the commands, and \cmd{cge} displays the version. The
Mata library is compiled the first time a command runs in a session.

\section{This manual}

Part~\ref{part:intro} introduces the package: a first model
(chapter~\ref{chap:start}), the workflow and its principles
(chapter~\ref{chap:workflow}), a worked example from a raw SAM to results
(chapter~\ref{chap:example}), and how the results are validated, how fast the
solver is and what to do when something fails (chapter~\ref{chap:validation}).
Part~\ref{part:model} states the model: its notation, equations, closures and
calibration, the solution method, the checks, the reporting measures, the
extensions, and the methods of the commands that are not part of the model
itself. Part~\ref{part:ref} is the reference for each command, in the form of
Stata's own manuals: syntax, options, examples with their output, stored
results and methods.

Commands typed in Stata are shown after a prompt, \cmd{. cge_solve}; output
follows them as Stata displays it. The examples use the SYNTHETIC SAMs that come
with the package: they are constructed for testing and describe no country, so
the results of the examples illustrate the commands and are not findings about
any economy. Mathematical symbols for the model's variables are written in
capitals ($\V{PQ}_c$, $\V{QA}_a$), and parameters in lower case ($\V{tm}_c$) or
Greek ($\sigma^{va}_a$); the names are those of the commands' results.

\section{How the package is tested}

The equations are implemented three times: in Stata and Mata (the package); in
Python, a reference implementation that writes the expected results of the
tests; and in GAMS, written from the specification and solved with PATH. The
Stata test suite compares every result with the Python reference, and the GAMS
implementation reproduces the same cases. The measures of the survey link are
checked against Stata's own commands, such as \cmd{xtile}, \cmd{_pctile} and
\cmd{svy}, and against their formulas. Chapter~\ref{chap:validation} gives
the details.
