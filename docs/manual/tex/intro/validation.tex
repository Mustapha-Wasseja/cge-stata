\chapter{Validation, performance and troubleshooting}\label{chap:validation}

\section{How results are validated}

\textbf{Three implementations.} The equations of Part~\ref{part:model} are
implemented in Stata and Mata, in a Python reference model and in GAMS. The
Python model runs in continuous integration and writes the expected results of
every test case. The Stata test suite, 63 test files, compares every variable
of every case with them and requires agreement to $10^{-6}$; in practice they
agree to about $10^{-11}$ or better. The GAMS model was written from the
specification, calibrates the model from the SAM and the user's settings alone,
and is solved with PATH; it reproduces the 186 scenarios of the Python
reference, including scenarios with constraints solved as complementarity
problems, to $2.9 \times 10^{-11}$ or better. \manref{cge_gams} writes any model
for GAMS, the built-in model with that program and a standalone model as a
program of its own: 67 exported cases, solved with PATH (17 of them also with
CONOPT, KNITRO and IPOPT, three nonlinear programmes with IPOPT and two
mixed-integer programmes with SCIP), reproduce the solutions of Stata to
$3.6 \times 10^{-9}$ or better, and to $2.1 \times 10^{-11}$ or better with
every solver but CONOPT (\secref{sec:gams-import}). The suite passes in
Stata~13.1 and in StataNow~19.5, and also runs every example do-file.

\textbf{The survey link and the user language.} The measures of
\manref{cge_microsim} are checked against Stata's own commands and against
their formulas. Quantile groups and percentiles are identical to those of
\cmd{xtile} and \cmd{_pctile}. Standard errors agree with \cmd{svy: ratio},
\cmd{lincom} and \cmd{svy, subpop()} to $10^{-10}$. Poverty, inequality,
concentration and the indexes of fiscal incidence agree with direct
computations to $10^{-12}$, and the Palma and S80/S20 ratios with closed
forms. The welfare of households that follow their group is that of
\manref{cge_report}, under Cobb--Douglas and LES demand. The contributions of
the Shapley decomposition add up to the change in every measure, and
reweighting meets its targets to $10^{-10}$. User equations with sets,
parameters and conditions are written into the GAMS program like the model's
own and agree with Stata's solutions (export check cases \cmd{user},
\cmd{usersets} and \cmd{conditions}); user inequalities, solved by GAMS as a
mixed complementarity problem, agree too (case \cmd{inequalities}), and
reproduce the constraints of \manref{cge_constraint} that they restate, as do
variables and equations with two indexes and sets of pairs (case
\cmd{pairs}). Sums over two indexes and over sets of pairs equal the nested
sums and the terms they stand for to $10^{-12}$. Lags take the values of the
periods before in every period of a dynamic run, to $10^{-12}$, and GAMS
reproduces a run with lags period by period (case \cmd{dynlag}). A built-in
equation written again in the user's language, in place of the original, leaves
the solution unchanged to $10^{-10}$, and GAMS leaves out the elements that user
equations replace (case \cmd{replace}). An objective written as an
expression gives the optimum of the report measure that it equals (to
$10^{-10}$), and a binding constraint of \manref{cge_optimize} gives the
instruments that reach it as a target (to $10^{-6}$). The equation listing of
\manref{cge_listing} labels every element as the model's own equations do: with
the tariffs removed at the base, it finds the import prices and government
revenue off by the tariffs, element by element. The functions
\cmd{max()}, \cmd{min()}, \cmd{abs()}, \cmd{sqr()}, \cmd{log10()} and
\cmd{ifthen()}, the products and extremes over sets, \cmd{card()} and macros
give the values of the same formulas computed in Stata from the results, to
$10^{-12}$; their exact derivatives agree with central differences away from
the kinks, and GAMS solves a model that uses each of them to Stata's solution
(case \cmd{functions}). Bounds that do not bind, scales and starting levels
leave the solution unchanged to $10^{-10}$, in Stata and in GAMS (cases
\cmd{bounds} and \cmd{boundscon}). The marginals of the constraints in the
solution listing are their adjusting variables' moves, and GAMS, which now
finds each regime itself, reports the same room and marginal for every pair in
the constraint cases. The shadow prices and reduced costs of
\manref{cge_optimize} agree with the change in the optimum when a bound or
target moves, to $10^{-5}$. Each scenario of a loop of \manref{cge_loop} is
the solution of its shocks solved on their own, to $10^{-12}$, from the
solution before or from the base, and GAMS, solving the scenarios in order,
reproduces every one (case \cmd{loop}). A GDX file written by \manref{cge_gdx}
and read back gives the SAM, the calibrated parameters and the levels of the
variables unchanged, to the last bit; the solution of a scenario solved by GAMS,
read from its own GDX file, agrees with Stata's to $10^{-13}$; and a SAM read
from that file calibrates the same model (\texttt{tools/gams/run\_gdx\_check.py}).
Sets defined by set algebra have the elements found by enumeration, and
conditions evaluated at each solve give, after a shock, the model whose base
meets them, to $10^{-14}$; GAMS solves the same elements (cases
\cmd{condsolve} and \cmd{sacond}). A variable added to an element of the model's own equations
gives the solution of the element replaced by its own equation with the
variable on its right side, to $10^{-10}$ (case \cmd{addterm}).

\textbf{Standalone models and solvers.} The standard model written whole in
the user language reproduces the built-in model to about $10^{-12}$, and GAMS
solves it to the same solution (case \cmd{standalone}). The semismooth
Newton method solves problems of MCPLIB with known solutions, Kojima and
Shindo's degenerate one among them, and agrees with PATH (cases
\cmd{mcpjosephy} and \cmd{mcptech}); box pairs reach each of their regimes,
and a tariff-rate quota whose cap does not bind is the quota as an
inequality (case \cmd{boxpair}). The interior point reproduces the published
optimum of problem 71 of \citet{hock1981}, with the marginals of its
equations against differences of their right sides, balances a matrix by
cross entropy to the RAS solution, and solves the counterexample of
\citet{wachter2000} with feasibility restoration, which reports a locally
infeasible programme as such. Its exact Hessians agree with central
differences of the exact gradients, with the same solutions and iterations.
Branch and bound reproduces enumeration of every integer point. The
experimental links (Stata 16 and later) are checked against the package's
own methods and published results: SuperLU against the sparse LU, HiGHS
against the interior point, IPOPT on problem 71 against the published
optimum to $3 \times 10^{-7}$ and the interior point's marginals to
$10^{-5}$, and SCIP against branch and bound and on a nonconvex polynomial,
where it finds the global minimum and the interior point a local one.
Reverse and forward differentiation give the same derivatives, against
central differences over every operation and function of the language, and
the exact Jacobians of linked regions and of runs with foresight agree with
central differences. Ruiz's equilibration leaves the solutions and
iterations unchanged; on a badly scaled test system it lowers the condition
number of the Jacobian from $5.4 \times 10^8$ to 3.9. \manref{cge_structure}
finds the blocks and the over- and under-determined parts of systems of
known structure, and a model solved block by block gives the solution of the
whole system to $1.3 \times 10^{-15}$; \manref{cge_iis} finds the one bound
among eight that makes a scenario infeasible. The reports and survey links
of standalone models give the price indexes, equivalent and compensating
variations and poverty computed directly from the solutions, to
$10^{-10}$, with decompositions that add up; their sensitivity analyses give
the prices and variations that each draw of the parameters implies, to
$10^{-8}$.

\textbf{Models in the form of MPSGE.} Five models written for GAMS's own
MPSGE (\texttt{tests/data/mpsge}), with taxes, nests at several depths and
indexed by a set, joint products, a free good, sectors that shut down or
start, auxiliary variables with an equal-yield tax and a floor on the real
wage, and report variables, are read from their GAMS programs by
\cmd{cge_mpsge using}, and four of them are also written in commands. Every
level of every scenario agrees with GAMS/MPSGE to about $10^{-13}$, and GAMS
solves the exported models to Stata's solutions with PATH (cases
\cmd{mpsge}, \cmd{mpsnest}, \cmd{mpsaux} and \cmd{mpsinest}).

\textbf{Checks in every run.} Calibration checks benchmark replication;
\manref{cge_check} adds SAM balance, a square closure, homogeneity, a perturbed
start, and, for a simulation, its residual, Walras's law and the GDP identity
(chapter~\ref{chap:checks}). Run it after building a model and after changing
the closure.

\section{Performance}\label{sec:performance}

The solver works on a reduced system with an exact Jacobian and Broyden's
updates (chapter~\ref{chap:solution}). On a Windows laptop, synthetic SAMs of
increasing size solved a removal of all import tariffs (elasticities 0.8, 2
and 2) in these times, in Stata 13.1/IC and in StataNow/MP 19.5 on four cores
with version 0.7.0, and in GAMS with PATH:

\midrule\endhead
\bottomrule\endlastfoot
40 & 95 & 1,814 & 711 & 0.5 & 0.5 & 1.8\\
80 & 180 & 5,446 & 1,407 & 2.6 & 1.8 & 1.8\\

The GAMS time is the whole run: GAMS calibrates the model from the SAM, solves
the base and then the scenario (version 0.6.3; with version 0.7.0 the GAMS
solutions agree with Stata's to $3 \times 10^{-9}$ or better). Times vary from
run to run with other work on the machine, by as much as a factor of two;
they show orders of magnitude.

Until version 0.7.0 most of Stata's time went into evaluating the model's
equations to build the Jacobian by finite differences, about four fifths of
the solve of the 200-activity model. The exact Jacobian and Broyden's updates
of version 0.7.0 cut the solve of the 250-activity model from 96 seconds to
27--32 in Stata 13.1/IC, and from 72 to 27--29 in StataNow/MP 19.5 (the GAMS
times are those of version 0.6.3, for the same models). The dense LU factorisation
of the Jacobian is now most of a large solve: in Stata 13.1/IC, 11.4 of the
seconds for the 250-activity model, against 3.6 for building four exact
Jacobians. GAMS uses a sparse solver, and its time barely grows over this
range. For models with several thousand equations in the reduced system,
export the scenario to GAMS and bring the solution back with \cmd{cge_gams
..., import()} (\secref{sec:gams-import}); the solution is checked against
the model's equations in Stata. Calibration and mapping are fast compared with solving.
Stata/IC holds at most 2,048 variables, but \manref{cge_sam} reads larger SAMs
directly from CSV files or from long data.

\cmd{linsolver(sparse)} (\secref{sec:sparse}), the same models solved in
these times in Stata 13.1/IC: the reduced system with the dense and with the
sparse LU, and the full system with the sparse LU, with the largest relative

The dense Jacobian of the full system of the 250-activity model would need 13
gigabytes, and that of the 400-activity model 71; with the sparse LU they are
solved in under a minute and in about two minutes. On the reduced systems the
two linear solvers take about the same time: their Jacobians have some twenty
entries a row, and building them and evaluating the equations take most of a
solve. A first form of the sparse LU, in the full systems with 80 activities
or more, took as pivots entries that rows cancelling in the elimination had
left at about $10^{-15}$ of their column; Newton's method then failed, and its
fallback, central differences with a dense Jacobian, took two minutes at 120
activities and ran out of memory at 250. Such columns now go to the dense
block, and the fallbacks use the sparse LU too; the test suite has a matrix
that fails without the check.

\section{Common problems}

\begin{cgetable}{p{0.36\linewidth}p{0.60\linewidth}}
\toprule
Symptom & Likely cause and remedy\\
\midrule\endhead
\bottomrule\endlastfoot
\cmd{cge_map} exits with error 459 and a table of problems & cells the model does not represent, unbalanced accounts, or accounts without roles: map the accounts differently, use a layout option (section~\ref{sec:layouts}), or correct the SAM\\
``activity output does not equal its sales'' & an activity's column total differs from its row of sales: balance the SAM, or check that exports and home consumption are recorded as the model expects\\
``every activity needs positive value added'' & an activity without factor payments: merge it with another activity or remove it\\
benchmark replication fails & an error in the SAM or the mapping that the checks did not catch; check negative cells, zero sales and the elasticities (for example \cmd{sigmaq(1)} for an imported commodity is not allowed)\\
``closure is not square'' & a closure fixes too many or too few variables, for example a factor closure that the model's data cannot support\\
\cmd{cge_solve} exits with error 430 & the shock has no solution, or one too far from the base: read the diagnostics (the equations with the largest residuals, and the smallest pivot), try more steps with \cmd{steps()}, check the closure, and look at \cmd{trace}\\
``the closure is singular'' & a swap leaves an equation with no free variable: free a variable of that equation, or undo the swap\\
a standalone model's Jacobian is singular & \manref{cge_structure} names the equations and variables of the over- and under-determined parts: fix a variable, free one, or add or remove an equation\\
a scenario with bounds or constraints does not solve & \manref{cge_iis} finds a set of them that cannot hold together, every one of which is needed for the failure\\
a nonlinear programme ends with model status 5 & the equations and bounds cannot hold together near the point where the interior point stopped: check the bounds, or start elsewhere with \cmd{starts()}\\
\cmd{solver(ipopt)}, \cmd{solver(scip)} or \cmd{solver(highs)} stops with error 198 & the links need Stata 16 or later with Python and SciPy, and cyipopt or PySCIPOpt: install them, or use the package's own solvers\\
\cmd{cge_mpsge end} stops with an error & the model is not added, and the statements are kept: correct the one named, or start again with \cmd{cge_mpsge, drop}\\
a shock ``is not fixed under the current closure'' & the variable is endogenous under the closure: change the closure, or shock a parameter\\
a negative or missing change in a report & percentage changes of negative bases (a deficit) are relative to the absolute value; a zero base gives a missing change\\
Stata 13 cannot install from GitHub & its TLS version is too old: install from a downloaded copy (chapter~\ref{chap:intro})\\
Stata 13 cannot read a saved model & the model was saved in a later version of Stata, whose Mata file format Stata 13 cannot read: save it in Stata 13\\
\end{cgetable}
