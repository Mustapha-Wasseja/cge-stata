\chapter{Validation, performance and troubleshooting}\label{chap:validation}

\section{How results are validated}

\textbf{Three implementations.} The equations of Part~\ref{part:model} are
implemented in Stata and Mata, in a Python reference model and in GAMS. The
Python model runs in continuous integration and writes the expected results of
every test case. The Stata test suite, 25 test files, compares every variable
of every case with them and requires agreement to $10^{-6}$; in practice they
agree to about $10^{-11}$ or better. The GAMS model was written from the
specification, calibrates the model from the SAM and the user's settings alone,
and is solved with PATH; it reproduces the 186 scenarios of the Python
reference, including scenarios with constraints solved as complementarity
problems, to $2.9 \times 10^{-11}$ or better. \manref{cge_gams} writes any model
for that GAMS program: 31 exported cases, solved with PATH, CONOPT, KNITRO and
IPOPT, reproduce the solutions of Stata to $4 \times 10^{-9}$ or better
(\secref{sec:gams-import}). The test suite also runs every example do-file.

\textbf{Checks in every run.} Calibration checks benchmark replication;
\manref{cge_check} adds SAM balance, a square closure, homogeneity, a perturbed
start, and, for a simulation, its residual, Walras's law and the GDP identity
(chapter~\ref{chap:checks}). Run it after building a model and after changing
the closure.

\section{Performance}\label{sec:performance}

The solver works on a reduced system and computes the Jacobian by groups of
columns (chapter~\ref{chap:solution}). On a Windows laptop, synthetic SAMs of
increasing size solved a removal of all import tariffs (elasticities 0.8, 2
and 2) in these times, in Stata 13.1/IC, in StataNow/MP 19.5 on four cores, and
in GAMS with PATH (version 0.6.3):

The GAMS time is the whole run: GAMS calibrates the model from the SAM, solves
the base and then the scenario. Times vary from run to run with other work on
the machine, by as much as 40 per cent; they show orders of magnitude.

Most of Stata's time goes into evaluating the model's equations to build the
Jacobian by finite differences: with the central differences used before
version 0.6.3, about four fifths of the solve of the 200-activity model in
Stata 13.1/IC. The one-sided differences of version 0.6.3 halve these
evaluations, and shortened that solve by about a third in Stata 13.1/IC and by
almost half in StataNow/MP. Four cores of StataNow/MP made the solve about 1.6
times faster than one core. GAMS uses analytic derivatives and a sparse
solver, and its time barely grows over this range. For models with more than
about 2,000 equations in the reduced system, export the scenario to GAMS and
bring the solution back with \cmd{cge_gams ..., import()}
(\secref{sec:gams-import}); the solution is checked against the model's
equations in Stata. Calibration and mapping are fast compared with solving.
Stata/IC holds at most 2,048 variables, but \manref{cge_sam} reads larger SAMs
directly from CSV files or from long data.

\section{Common problems}

\begin{cgetable}{p{0.36\linewidth}p{0.60\linewidth}}
\toprule
Symptom & Likely cause and remedy\\
\midrule\endhead
\bottomrule\endlastfoot
\cmd{cge_map} exits with error 459 and a table of problems & cells the model does not represent, unbalanced accounts, or accounts without roles: map the accounts differently, use a layout option (section~\ref{sec:layouts}), or correct the SAM\\
``activity output does not equal its sales'' & an activity's column total differs from its row of sales: balance the SAM, or check that exports and home consumption are recorded as the model expects\\
``every activity needs positive value added'' & an activity without factor payments: merge it with another activity or remove it\\
benchmark replication fails & an error in the SAM or the mapping that the checks did not catch; check negative cells, zero sales and the elasticities (for example \cmd{sigmaq(1)} for an imported commodity is not allowed)\\
``closure is not square'' & a closure fixes too many or too few variables, for example a factor closure that the model's data cannot support\\
\cmd{cge_solve} exits with error 430 & the shock has no solution, or one too far from the base: read the diagnostics (the equations with the largest residuals, and the smallest pivot), try more steps with \cmd{steps()}, check the closure, and look at \cmd{trace}\\
``the closure is singular'' & a swap leaves an equation with no free variable: free a variable of that equation, or undo the swap\\
a shock ``is not fixed under the current closure'' & the variable is endogenous under the closure: change the closure, or shock a parameter\\
a negative or missing change in a report & percentage changes of negative bases (a deficit) are relative to the absolute value; a zero base gives a missing change\\
Stata 13 cannot install from GitHub & its TLS version is too old: install from a downloaded copy (chapter~\ref{chap:intro})\\
Stata 13 cannot read a saved model & the model was saved in a later version of Stata, whose Mata file format Stata 13 cannot read: save it in Stata 13\\
\end{cgetable}
