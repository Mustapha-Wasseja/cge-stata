\section{Welfare}\label{sec:welfare}

The welfare of household $h$ is measured with its expenditure function,
\begin{equation*}
E_h(\V{PQ}, u) = \sum_c \V{PQ}_c\,\gamma_{ch} + u \prod_c \V{PQ}_c^{\beta_{ch}},
\end{equation*}
which is that of Cobb--Douglas demand when $\gamma = 0$ (with VAT, $\V{PQ}$
stands for $\V{PH}$, and home goods are valued at $\V{PA}_a$). With base
spending $\V{EH}^0_h$ and new spending $\V{EH}_h$, the equivalent variation is
the change in spending at base prices that gives the new utility, and the
compensating variation the change in spending at new prices that would restore
base utility:
\begin{align*}
\V{EV}_h &= \sum_c \V{PQ}^0_c\,\gamma_{ch} + \Bigl(\V{EH}_h - \sum_c \V{PQ}_c\,\gamma_{ch}\Bigr)
  \prod_c \Bigl(\frac{\V{PQ}^0_c}{\V{PQ}_c}\Bigr)^{\beta_{ch}} - \V{EH}^0_h,\\
\V{CV}_h &= \V{EH}_h - \sum_c \V{PQ}_c\,\gamma_{ch} - \Bigl(\V{EH}^0_h - \sum_c \V{PQ}^0_c\,\gamma_{ch}\Bigr)
  \prod_c \Bigl(\frac{\V{PQ}_c}{\V{PQ}^0_c}\Bigr)^{\beta_{ch}}.
\end{align*}
Both are positive for a gain and are reported in per cent of $\V{EH}^0_h$.
Neither values public goods, the environment or leisure.

\section{Changes}

Reports express a measure $x$ as a change from its base value $x^0$: rates in
per cent (public debt in per cent of GDP, unemployment rates, the share of
time lost to illness, the death rate) change by $x - x^0$ percentage points;
other measures by $100\,(x - x^0)/|x^0|$ per cent, so that a fall is negative
even when the base is negative, as for a government deficit. A change from a
base of zero is missing.
