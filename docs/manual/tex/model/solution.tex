\chapter{The solution method}\label{chap:solution}

\manref{cge_solve} solves the system of equations of
chapter~\ref{chap:equations} under the closure of chapter~\ref{chap:closures}.
The variables of all blocks are stacked in one vector. Variables that do not
exist for an element (for example the imports of a commodity that is not
imported) stay in the vector at zero and are left out of the system, and so
are the variables that the closure fixes. The free variables $x$ and the
equations $r(x) = 0$ form a square system.

\section{Newton's method}\label{sec:newton}

The solver works on scaled variables and scaled residuals,
\begin{equation*}
z_j = x_j / s_j, \qquad F_i(z) = r_i(x) / e_i,
\end{equation*}
where $s_j$ and $e_i$ are the base magnitudes of the variable and of the
equation (one where the base value is zero), so that the tolerance applies
alike to every block. Starting from the base, each Newton step solves
\begin{equation*}
J(z)\,\Delta z = -F(z)
\end{equation*}
by the LU factorisation of the Jacobian $J$, and the new point is
$z + \lambda\,\Delta z$, with $\lambda$ the largest of $1, \tfrac12, \tfrac14,
\dots$ (down to $10^{-8}$) such that
\begin{equation*}
\lVert F(z + \lambda\,\Delta z)\rVert \le (1 - 10^{-4}\lambda)\,\lVert F(z)\rVert,
\end{equation*}
a backtracking line search on the Euclidean norm of the residuals. Points at
which a price or quantity is not positive are rejected, and the step is
shortened. The solve has converged when $\max_i |F_i| < \tau$, the tolerance
(\texttt{1e-10} by default), over every equation of the model, including the
definitions left out of the reduced system (section~\ref{sec:reduced}).

From version 0.7.0, after each Newton step, full or shortened by the line
search, further steps use Broyden's updates \citep{broyden1965}. With $s$ the
step and $y = F(z + s) - F(z)$, Broyden's good method replaces the Jacobian
$B$ by
\begin{equation*}
B_+ = B + \frac{(y - B s)\, s'}{s' s},
\end{equation*}
which agrees with the change in the residuals along the step. By the
Sherman--Morrison formula its inverse $H$ becomes
\begin{equation*}
H_+ = (I + a\, s')\, H, \qquad a = \frac{s - H y}{s' H y}
\end{equation*}
\citep[chapter~7]{kelley1995}, so $H$ is kept as the LU factors of the last
Jacobian times the factors $(I + a_k s_k')$ of the updates since, and the next
direction is $d_+ = -H_+ F(z + s)$. As $H F(z) = -d$ for the direction $d$ of
the last step, $H y = H F(z + s) + d$ needs a single solve with the LU
factors: a Broyden step costs one evaluation of the equations, two triangular
solves and a few inner products. It is taken with a line search of at most
two halvings; a new Jacobian is computed when that fails, after a step that
reduces $\lVert F\rVert$ by less than a tenth, and after 30 updates. Newton's
method with a new Jacobian at each step converges quadratically near a
solution, and Broyden's method superlinearly, with far fewer Jacobians and
factorisations; far from a solution, where the line search shortens the
Newton steps, the updates also save Jacobians. Before version 0.7.0 the
factors of a Jacobian were reused without updates while each full step at
least halved $\lVert F\rVert$, a chord method, which converges only
linearly.

Once the tolerance is met, up to three more steps with the same factors are
taken while each halves $\lVert F\rVert$, toward a thousandth of the
tolerance. A solution far inside the tolerance varies smoothly with the
shocks, which \manref{cge_optimize} and \manref{cge_sensitivity} rely on.
\manref{cge_optimize}, whose solves start from the solution before, takes
steps with the factors but without Broyden's updates: from a nearby start a
Newton step and such steps reach far below the tolerance.

\section{The reduced system}\label{sec:reduced}

Some equations are explicit definitions: a variable equals an expression in
other variables. Such definitions are left out of the Newton system, and their
variables are computed from the other variables before each evaluation of the
residuals. They are \eqref{eq:P1}, \eqref{eq:P2} and \eqref{eq:P6} (import and
export prices and the price of intermediate input), \eqref{eq:Q5}
(intermediate demand), \eqref{eq:Q6} (output by commodity), \eqref{eq:I5}
(household demand), \eqref{eq:T8} (demand for margin services),
\eqref{eq:I13} (the purchases of the health insurance fund), \eqref{eq:H2}
(factor efficiency) and \eqref{eq:H3} (days worked). With VAT, government
revenue \eqref{eq:I8} depends on intermediate and household demand and on the
fund's purchases, and \eqref{eq:P6}, \eqref{eq:Q5}, \eqref{eq:I5} and
\eqref{eq:I13} stay in the system. A definition is left out only when its
equation elements are exactly the existing elements of its variable.
Variables computed from their definitions cannot be fixed by the closure
(\secref{sec:swaps}).

The left-out equations still hold exactly at every point. Intermediate and
household demand grow with the square of the number of sectors, so most of a
large model's equations are left out: for a SAM with 250 activities and 20
households, 4,254 of 40,190 equations remain. \cmd{cge_solve, full} keeps
every equation; the test suite checks that both systems give the same
solution. The pattern of the reduced system is found by substituting each
left-out variable by the variables of its definition.

\section{The Jacobian}\label{sec:jacobian}

From version 0.7.0 the Jacobian is exact. The derivatives of every equation
block of chapter~\ref{chap:equations} are written out from its equation, as
entries $(i, j, \partial r_i / \partial x_j)$ of a sparse matrix; an entry
that arises more than once, such as the derivative of the value of composite
supply \eqref{eq:P3} with respect to the commodity's own price, which enters
the equation in several terms, is summed. The entries are scaled as the system,
$J_{ij} = (\partial r_i / \partial x_j)\, s_j / e_i$, and written into a dense
matrix for the LU factorisation.

In the reduced system the variables of the left-out definitions are
eliminated by the chain rule. Each left-out definition is an equation
$D_k(d_k, y) = 0$ in its variable $d_k$ and other variables $y$, such as
$\mathit{QINT}_{ca} - \mathit{ica}_{ca}\,\mathit{QINTA}_a = 0$ or, for household
demand \eqref{eq:I5}, $\mathit{PH}_c\, \mathit{QH}_{ch} - \mathit{PH}_c\,
\gamma_{ch} - \beta_{ch}\, \mathit{SN}_h = 0$. Its derivatives give
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\partial d_k}{\partial y_j} = -\frac{\partial D_k / \partial y_j}{\partial D_k / \partial d_k},
\end{equation*}
and each entry of another row with respect to $d_k$ is replaced by its
products with these derivatives:
\begin{equation*}
\frac{d r_i}{d y_j} = \frac{\partial r_i}{\partial y_j} + \sum_k \frac{\partial r_i}{\partial d_k}\, \frac{\partial d_k}{\partial y_j}.
\end{equation*}
No left-out definition depends on another, so one substitution suffices.

User equations (chapter~\ref{chap:user}) are differentiated by forward
differentiation of their compiled postfix form: each value on the stack
carries its derivatives with respect to the variables of the equation, which
the operators combine by the rules of the derivative, for example $(ab)' =
a'b + ab'$, $(a/b)' = a'/b - a b'/b^2$, $(a^b)' = a^b (b\, a'/a + \ln a\,
b')$ (or $b\, a^{b-1} a'$ for an exponent without variables) and $(\ln a)' =
a'/a$. The rows of constraints \eqref{eq:B1} have exact derivatives too.

The test suite checks the exact Jacobian against central differences taken
column by column, for the reduced and the full system, at the base, at
solutions and at points with every free variable perturbed by up to 3 per
cent, in every SAM layout and with every feature of the model; the largest
difference, about $10^{-7}$, is the error of the differences themselves. Building the
Jacobian takes one pass over its entries, where finite differences needed an
evaluation of all the equations for each group of columns (67 groups for the
200-activity model of \secref{sec:performance}).

Finite differences remain the fallback. Before version 0.7.0 the Jacobian
was built by them for the rows with few variables, over groups of columns
that share no row \citep{curtis1974}: the columns are coloured greedily, the
most connected first, so that one evaluation of the equations gives the
derivatives of every column of a group. For a group $G$, with $e_G$ the
vector with ones in the columns of $G$,
\begin{equation*}
J_{ij} \approx \frac{F_i(z + h \odot e_G) - F_i(z - h \odot e_G)}{2 h_j}, \qquad j \in G,
\qquad h_j = 10^{-6} \max(1, |z_j|),
\end{equation*}
central differences, whose error is about $10^{-10}$; version 0.6.3 used
one-sided differences, with $h_j = 10^{-7} \max(1, |z_j|)$ and one
evaluation for each group. When Newton's method fails with the exact
Jacobian, it is tried again from the start with central differences and
without Broyden's updates.

\section{The sparse LU}\label{sec:sparse}

Each Newton step solves $J\,\Delta z = -F$. By default the Jacobian is held as
a dense matrix and factorised by Mata's LU with partial pivoting, in time
proportional to $n^3$ and memory to $n^2$ for $n$ equations. From version
0.10.1, \manref{cge_options}, \cmd{linsolver(sparse)}, factorises it as a
sparse matrix, in the manner of the analyse-and-factorise solvers of
\citet{duff1986}; its memory grows with the number of entries.

\textit{Analysis.} The rows with more than ten times the average number of
entries, or 50, are set apart: the sums of government revenue and spending,
the balances and the price indexes. On the others, the pivots are chosen by
Markowitz's rule with threshold pivoting \citep{markowitz1957}: at each step,
among the active columns with the fewest entries (from a pool of 64 chosen
again every 16 steps, four at most), the entries $a_{ij}$ with
$|a_{ij}| \ge 0.1 \max_k |a_{kj}|$, the one that minimises
$(r_i - 1)(c_j - 1)$, with $r_i$ and $c_j$ the active entries of its row and
column, is the pivot, and the elimination of its row from the others gives
the pattern of the factors, fill included. Rows that cancel in the
elimination leave in a column only entries lost to rounding; a column whose
active entries are all below $10^{-10}$ of its largest entry in the rows not
set apart is therefore left over, and its pivot comes from the rows set
apart. A row left without a pivot joins those set apart. The analysis gives
orders $p$ and $q$ of the rows and columns, and the patterns of $L$ and $U$
for the sparse block.

\textit{Factorisation.} With $B = A[p, q]$ partitioned into the sparse block
$A_{11}$ and the rows set apart and columns left over,
\begin{equation*}
B = \begin{pmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12}\\ A_{21} & A_{22} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
A_{11} = L U, \qquad S = A_{22} - A_{21} A_{11}^{-1} A_{12},
\end{equation*}
$L U$ is computed column by column (left-looking), each column of $U$ by
the columns of $L$ before it in the pattern of the analysis. $A_{12}$ and
$A_{21}$ are kept as entries, and the Schur complement $S$, small and dense,
is formed from $A_{11}^{-1} A_{12}$ a group of columns at a time, so that the
memory stays proportional to the entries, and factorised by Mata's LU. A
system $B y = c$ is solved as $y_2 = S^{-1}(c_2 - A_{21} A_{11}^{-1} c_1)$,
$y_1 = A_{11}^{-1}(c_1 - A_{12} y_2)$. Each later Jacobian with the same
pattern, or with entries only within it, is factorised in the same order;
when a pivot falls below $10^{-8}$ times the largest entry of its column of
$L$, or below $10^{-10}$ times the largest entry of its column of $A_{11}$,
the analysis is made again with the current values. The analysis is kept for later solves of the same model, so
loops, sensitivity analyses, dynamic runs and the optimiser pay it once.
Broyden's updates apply to the sparse factors as to the dense ones, and the
fallbacks of section~\ref{sec:continuation} with central or one-sided
differences use the sparse LU too, with the differences of each group of
columns as entries. Powell's hybrid method, which holds the Jacobian dense,
is left out with the sparse LU above 10,000 equations.

\section{Fallbacks and continuation}\label{sec:continuation}

If Newton's method fails with the exact Jacobian and with central
differences, Powell's hybrid method is tried (Mata's \cmd{solvenl()}, technique \cmd{broydenpowell}),
followed by Newton steps to polish the result.

If that fails too, the shock is applied by continuation: the model is solved
for $k = 1, \dots, n$ with parameters and fixed values
\begin{equation*}
\theta_k = \theta^0 + \frac{k}{n}\,(\theta^1 - \theta^0),
\end{equation*}
between their base values $\theta^0$ and their shocked values $\theta^1$,
each step starting from the solution of the step before; by default in
$n = 4$ steps and then in $n = 16$. A shock that defeats continuation is often
one that the economy cannot absorb.

\section{Diagnostics}\label{sec:diagnostics}

A solution of the equations need not be an equilibrium that makes economic
sense. From version 0.7.0, after a solve that converges, \manref{cge_solve}
notes any quantity, price, income or scaling factor of demand that is
negative or below one millionth of its positive base value, and stores their
number in \res{r(N_collapsed)}. With savings-driven investment, for example,
a tenfold rise in government purchases is absorbed by negative investment,
with the output of two activities near zero (\manref{cge_solve}).

After a failure, or on request, \manref{cge_solve} displays:
\begin{itemize}
\item the equations with the largest scaled residuals at the last point, by
block and element;
\item any equation with no free variable, and any free variable in no
equation, which make the Jacobian singular;
\item the smallest pivot of the LU factorisation of the Jacobian relative to
the largest, and the variable it belongs to: partial pivoting permutes rows
only, so column $j$ of $U$ belongs to variable $j$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Constraints}\label{sec:solution-constraints}

With constraints (chapter~\ref{chap:constraints}) the model is solved in two
stages: first with smoothed complementarity conditions, which find whether each
constraint binds, and then with the exact conditions in the regimes found,
changing the regime of any constraint whose other condition fails, for at most
ten rounds (\secref{sec:cons-solution}).

\section{Other implementations}

The Python reference implementation uses the same Newton method with a
backtracking line search, and its own pure active-set method for constraints.
The GAMS program written by \manref{cge_gams} is solved by the solvers that
GAMS provides, PATH by default (chapter~\ref{chap:gams}). Their solutions agree
with those of \cmd{cge_solve} to the precision of the solutions
(chapter~\ref{chap:validation}).
