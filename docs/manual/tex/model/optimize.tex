\chapter{Policy optimisation}\label{chap:optimize}

\manref{cge_optimize} chooses the values $u$ of a few instruments to maximise or
minimise a report measure $f$ (chapter~\ref{chap:reporting}), or to reach
targets $g_j(u) = \bar g_j$ for report measures, with the model solved at every
trial value:
\begin{equation*}
\max_u\; f\bigl(x(u), u\bigr) \quad \text{subject to} \quad
g_j\bigl(x(u), u\bigr) = \bar g_j, \qquad \underline u \le u \le \overline u,
\end{equation*}
where $x(u)$ solves the model under the current closure. An instrument is one
element of a parameter that \manref{cge_shock} changes, or of a variable that
the closure fixes.

\section{Algorithm}

Each trial solves the model by Newton's method from the solution of the trial
before. Instruments are measured as $u = u^0 + s \odot z$, with $s$ the scale of
each instrument.

\textbf{Targets alone}, as many as instruments: the residuals
$r_j = (g_j - \bar g_j)/|\bar g_j|$ are solved by Newton's method, with a
Jacobian from central differences (step $10^{-5}\max(1, |z_k|)$ in $z$) and steps halved
until the residuals fall.

\textbf{An objective}: a projected Newton method maximises $f / \max(1,
|f^0|)$. The gradient and Hessian come from central differences, an
instrument at a bound whose gradient points outwards is held there, steps are
projected on the bounds and halved until the objective rises, and the
iterations stop when the gradient of the free instruments is below $10^{-8}$.

\textbf{An objective with targets}: the targets are equality constraints, met
by the augmented Lagrangian method. The maximiser takes
\begin{equation*}
f - \lambda' r - \tfrac{\rho}{2}\,r'r,
\end{equation*}
and the multipliers move by $\rho\,r$ after each round, $\lambda \leftarrow
\lambda + \rho\,r$, until $\max_j |r_j| < 10^{-10}$.

\section{Results}

The optimum is stored as a simulation and left as the current shocks. The
optimum may be local: a flat objective is located only to about $10^{-6}$ in
the instruments, and other starting values may lead elsewhere. The Python
reference solves the same problems with the SLSQP method of SciPy and with
least squares; the test suites compare the instruments (to $10^{-5}$), the
objective (to $10^{-9}$) and the solution, and check that a target for
government savings reached by the direct tax rate reproduces the closure
\cmd{fixgsav} (to $10^{-10}$).
