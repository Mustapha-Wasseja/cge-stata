\chapter{Linked regions}\label{chap:regions}

From version 0.12.0, \manref{cge_region} links calibrated single-country
models through bilateral trade, as multi-region models such as GTAP do
\citep{hertel1997}. Each region $r$ is a model of its own, from its own SAM,
with its own closure, parameters and shocks; the regions share world prices
and trade with each other and with the rest of the world.

\section{Routes}

The base trade between the regions is a set of routes: an exporter $s$, an
importer $r$ and a commodity $c$, with the value $V_{src}$ at world prices in
world units and, optionally, a tariff $t^0_{src}$, the importer's average
$tm^0_{rc}$ where none is given. A unit of the SAM of region $r$ is worth
$u_r$ world units, so its base imports of $c$ are worth
$M^0_{rc} = pwm^0_c\,\V{QM}^0_c\,u_r$ and its base exports
$E^0_{rc} = pwe^0_c\,(\V{QE}^0_c + \V{QER}^0_c)\,u_r$. The routes from the
other regions may not exceed $M^0_{rc}$, and those to them $E^0_{sc}$; the rest
of the world takes the difference, so that every region's trade of the
commodity is that of its SAM. The route of imports from the rest of the world
has the tariff that gives the importer's average:
\begin{equation*}
tm^0_{rc}\,M^0_{rc} = \sum_s t^0_{src}\,V_{src} + t^0_{wrc}\,V_{wrc}.
\end{equation*}

\section{Link variables and equations}

For each commodity $c$ that region $r$ imports from another region, its world
import price relative to the base, $\pi^M_{rc} = pwm_{rc}/pwm^0_{rc}$, and its
average tariff $tm_{rc}$ are unknowns; for each commodity that region $s$
exports to another region, its world export price relative to the base,
$\pi^E_{sc} = pwe_{sc}/pwe^0_{sc}$. They take the place of the parameters
$pwm_c$, $tm_c$ and $pwe_c$ in the region's equations. A route's price is the
exporter's $\pi^E_{sc}$, or the rest of the world's export price $\pi^W_c$ (1
at the base), and with its tariff, relative to the base,
$x_k = \pi_k\,(1 + t_k)/(1 + t^0_k)$. The imports of region $r$ are a CES
aggregate of the routes into it, with elasticity $\sigma_c$ and the base
shares $\theta_k = V_k(1 + t^0_k)/\sum_j V_j(1 + t^0_j)$:
\begin{gather*}
P_{rc} = \Bigl[\sum_k \theta_k\,x_k^{1-\sigma_c}\Bigr]^{1/(1-\sigma_c)}, \qquad
q_k = V_k\,\frac{\V{QM}_{rc}}{\V{QM}^0_{rc}}\,\Bigl(\frac{P_{rc}}{x_k}\Bigr)^{\sigma_c},
\end{gather*}
in world units at base prices. The exports of region $s$ to the rest of the
world follow its demand, $q_k = V_k\,(\pi^E_{sc}/\pi^D_c)^{-\eta_c}$, where
$\pi^D_c$ shifts it. The link equations are
\begin{align*}
\pi^M_{rc}\,(1 + tm_{rc}) &= (1 + tm^0_{rc})\,P_{rc},\\
tm_{rc}\,\pi^M_{rc}\,M^0_{rc}\,\frac{\V{QM}_{rc}}{\V{QM}^0_{rc}} &= \sum_k t_k\,\pi_k\,q_k,\\
E^0_{sc}\,\frac{\V{QE}_{sc} + \V{QER}_{sc}}{\V{QE}^0_{sc} + \V{QER}^0_{sc}} &= \sum_k q_k,
\end{align*}
the first two over the routes into $r$, the third over those out of $s$: the
tariff-inclusive price of the imports is their CES price index, the tariff
revenue that of the routes, and the exports of a region the sum of its
routes. With the first two, the value of the imports at world prices is that
of the routes. At the base every link variable holds its base value and the
regions their base solutions.

\section{Solution}

The linked system has the full system of each region and the link equations.
It is solved by Newton's method with a line search and the sparse LU
(section~\ref{sec:sparse}): each region's Jacobian is exact; the columns of the
link variables in a region's equations, and the link equations, are central
differences. Newton's method starts from the base, or from each region solved
on its own at the base links. Each region's solution is stored in its own
model with its link variables as its parameters $pwm_c$, $tm_c$ and $pwe_c$, so
it solves that model on its own, which the test suite checks; the routes are
kept with the links.

The test suite compares the solutions of two linked regions, the synthetic SAM
and its institutions layout, with a reference implementation in Python
(\texttt{reference/cge\_ref/regions.py}), which builds the routes and solves the
linked system with the Python reference models of the two regions: at the
base, with a free-trade area between them, with a higher price of
manufactures from the rest of the world, and with higher productivity in one
region, to $10^{-8}$ in every variable and route.
